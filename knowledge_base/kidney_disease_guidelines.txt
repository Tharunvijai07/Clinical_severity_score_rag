KIDNEY DISEASE CLINICAL GUIDELINES
KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) — Integrated Reference

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 1: ACUTE KIDNEY INJURY (AKI) — DEFINITION AND STAGING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

AKI is defined as any of the following (KDIGO 2012):
  • Increase in serum creatinine by ≥ 0.3 mg/dL (≥ 26.5 µmol/L) within 48 hours
  • Increase in serum creatinine to ≥ 1.5 times baseline within the prior 7 days
  • Urine volume < 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 6 hours

AKI STAGING (KDIGO):

Stage 1 — MILD AKI:
  • Serum creatinine: 1.5–1.9× baseline OR increase ≥ 0.3 mg/dL
  • Urine output: < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6–12 hours
  • Management: Identify and correct reversible causes; volume assessment

Stage 2 — MODERATE AKI:
  • Serum creatinine: 2.0–2.9× baseline
  • Urine output: < 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 12 hours
  • Management: Nephrology consultation; avoid nephrotoxins; close monitoring

Stage 3 — SEVERE AKI:
  • Serum creatinine: ≥ 3.0× baseline OR serum creatinine ≥ 4.0 mg/dL (354 µmol/L)
  • Urine output: < 0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥ 24 hours OR anuria for ≥ 12 hours
  • Initiation of renal replacement therapy (RRT)
  • Management: Urgent nephrology consultation; consider RRT; ICU-level care

CRITICAL CREATININE THRESHOLDS:
  • Creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL: Significant renal impairment — requires medical attention
  • Creatinine > 3.0 mg/dL: Severe AKI — hospital admission mandatory
  • Creatinine > 5.0 mg/dL: Critical — indicates severe renal dysfunction, dialysis often required
  • Creatinine > 8.0 mg/dL: Life-threatening — immediate nephrology and ICU involvement

BLOOD UREA NITROGEN (BUN) THRESHOLDS:
  • BUN > 20 mg/dL: Above normal; monitor closely
  • BUN > 40 mg/dL: Moderate azotemia; evaluate for renal impairment
  • BUN > 80 mg/dL: Severe azotemia; uremia risk
  • BUN > 100 mg/dL: Critical uremia — consider dialysis
  BUN/Creatinine ratio > 20:1 suggests pre-renal etiology

SECTION 2: CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE (CKD) — GFR STAGING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CKD STAGES (eGFR in mL/min/1.73m²):

G1 — Normal or high: eGFR ≥ 90 (with evidence of kidney damage)
G2 — Mildly decreased: eGFR 60–89
G3a — Mild to moderately decreased: eGFR 45–59
G3b — Moderately to severely decreased: eGFR 30–44
G4 — Severely decreased: eGFR 15–29 (prepare for renal replacement therapy)
G5 — Kidney failure: eGFR < 15 (dialysis or transplant required)

CKD SEVERITY BY eGFR:
  • eGFR > 60: Low severity — monitor annually
  • eGFR 30–59: Moderate severity — monitor every 3–6 months; restrict nephrotoxins
  • eGFR 15–29: High severity — refer to transplant program; manage complications
  • eGFR < 15: Critical — kidney failure; initiate renal replacement therapy

SECTION 3: ELECTROLYTE ABNORMALITIES IN KIDNEY DISEASE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

HYPERKALEMIA (High Potassium — K+):
  • Normal range: 3.5–5.0 mEq/L
  • Mild hyperkalemia: 5.1–5.9 mEq/L — dietary modification; close monitoring
  • Moderate hyperkalemia: 6.0–6.4 mEq/L — medical management; ECG monitoring required
  • Severe hyperkalemia: ≥ 6.5 mEq/L — LIFE-THREATENING; immediate treatment (calcium gluconate, insulin-dextrose, dialysis)
  • Potassium > 7.0 mEq/L: Cardiac arrest risk; emergency intervention mandatory
  ECG changes: peaked T waves → widened QRS → sine wave pattern → VF

HYPONATREMIA (Low Sodium — Na+):
  • Normal range: 136–145 mEq/L
  • Mild: Na+ 130–135 mEq/L
  • Moderate: Na+ 125–129 mEq/L — monitor carefully; correct underlying cause
  • Severe: Na+ < 125 mEq/L — symptomatic; risk of cerebral edema
  • Critical: Na+ < 120 mEq/L — emergency; hypertonic saline infusion

METABOLIC ACIDOSIS IN RENAL FAILURE:
  • Serum bicarbonate < 22 mEq/L: Mild acidosis
  • Serum bicarbonate < 18 mEq/L: Moderate acidosis; oral bicarbonate replacement
  • Serum bicarbonate < 15 mEq/L: Severe acidosis
  • pH < 7.3 with anion gap elevation: Anion gap metabolic acidosis — urgent evaluation
  • pH < 7.2: Severe acidemia — intensive intervention required
  • pH < 7.0: Critical acidemia — life-threatening; immediate mechanical ventilation consideration

PHOSPHATE ABNORMALITIES:
  • Hyperphosphatemia (>4.5 mg/dL): Common in CKD; contributes to secondary hyperparathyroidism
  • Phosphate > 6.0 mg/dL: Significant; dietary restriction and phosphate binders indicated
  • Calcium × Phosphate product > 55: Calcification risk

SECTION 4: URINALYSIS INDICATORS IN RENAL DISEASE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PROTEINURIA:
  • Urine protein/creatinine ratio (UPCR):
    - < 0.2: Normal
    - 0.2–0.5: Mild proteinuria — monitor
    - 0.5–3.5: Moderate to heavy proteinuria — nephrology referral
    - > 3.5 g/g (nephrotic range): Severe — nephrotic syndrome evaluation

  • 24-hour urine protein:
    - < 150 mg/day: Normal
    - 150–500 mg/day: Mild
    - 500–3500 mg/day: Moderate
    - > 3500 mg/day: Nephrotic range — aggressive management

HEMATURIA:
  • > 3 RBC/HPF on two of three urinalyses: Significant hematuria — evaluate for glomerulonephritis or malignancy
  • Dysmorphic RBCs or RBC casts: Glomerular bleeding — nephritic syndrome

CASTS:
  • RBC casts: Glomerulonephritis (critical finding)
  • WBC casts: Pyelonephritis or interstitial nephritis
  • Granular or muddy brown casts: Acute tubular necrosis (ATN)

SECTION 5: AKI INDICATIONS FOR DIALYSIS (AEIOU)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Emergency dialysis is indicated for:
  A — Acidosis: pH < 7.1 refractory to medical management
  E — Electrolytes: Life-threatening hyperkalemia (K+ > 6.5 mEq/L) unresponsive to treatment
  I — Intoxications: Certain toxic ingestions (methanol, ethylene glycol, lithium, salicylates)
  O — Overload: Volume overload causing pulmonary edema refractory to diuretics
  U — Uremia: BUN > 100 mg/dL with uremic encephalopathy, pericarditis, or bleeding
