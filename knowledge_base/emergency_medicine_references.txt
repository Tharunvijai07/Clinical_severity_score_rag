EMERGENCY MEDICINE REFERENCES
Critical Laboratory Values, Electrolyte Emergencies, and Rapid Clinical Decision Framework

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 1: CRITICAL LABORATORY VALUES (PANIC VALUES)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

These values require immediate physician notification and intervention.

COMPLETE BLOOD COUNT (CBC) CRITICAL VALUES:
  Hemoglobin:
    • < 7.0 g/dL: Transfusion consideration
    • < 5.0 g/dL: Critical anemia — immediate transfusion
    • > 20 g/dL: Polycythemia — hyperviscosity risk
  
  WBC:
    • < 2,000/µL: Severe leukopenia — neutropenic precautions; infection risk
    • < 500/µL (neutrophils): Profound neutropenia — reverse isolation; empiric antibiotics
    • > 100,000/µL: Extreme leukocytosis — leukemia vs. leukemoid reaction
  
  Platelets:
    • < 50,000/µL: Significant thrombocytopenia — bleeding risk
    • < 20,000/µL: Spontaneous bleeding risk — platelet transfusion
    • < 10,000/µL: Critical — prophylactic platelet transfusion

BASIC METABOLIC PANEL (BMP) CRITICAL VALUES:
  Sodium (Na+):
    • < 120 mEq/L: Critical hyponatremia — seizure/coma risk; hypertonic saline
    • > 160 mEq/L: Critical hypernatremia — cerebral desiccation; careful rehydration

  Potassium (K+):
    • < 2.5 mEq/L: Critical hypokalemia — arrhythmia risk; IV replacement
    • < 2.0 mEq/L: Life-threatening — rhabdomyolysis, respiratory failure
    • > 6.5 mEq/L: Critical hyperkalemia — cardiac arrest risk; immediate treatment
    • > 7.0 mEq/L: Extreme hyperkalemia — sine wave ECG; emergent dialysis
  
  Calcium (Ca2+):
    • < 6.5 mg/dL: Critical hypocalcemia — tetany, seizures, laryngospasm
    • > 13.0 mg/dL: Critical hypercalcemia — crisis; aggressive IV hydration
    • > 15.0 mg/dL: Hypercalcemic emergency — coma risk; dialysis consideration
  
  Glucose:
    • < 40 mg/dL: Critical hypoglycemia — brain injury risk; immediate IV dextrose
    • < 30 mg/dL: Severe hypoglycemia — potentially irreversible neuronal damage
    • > 500 mg/dL: Critical hyperglycemia — DKA or HHS
    • > 800 mg/dL: Extreme — HHS with coma risk
  
  Creatinine:
    • > 4.0 mg/dL: Severe renal failure — nephrology consultation urgent
    • > 10.0 mg/dL: Critical — dialysis usually indicated

  pH (Arterial):
    • < 7.2: Severe acidemia — evaluate etiology; consider intubation
    • < 7.0: Critical acidemia — life-threatening; immediate ICU
    • > 7.6: Severe alkalemia — evaluate cause; arrhythmia risk

  Bicarbonate (HCO3):
    • < 10 mEq/L: Critical metabolic acidosis — immediate evaluation
    • < 6 mEq/L: Extreme acidosis — imminent cardiac arrest
    • > 45 mEq/L: Critical metabolic alkalosis

  Lactate:
    • > 4.0 mmol/L: Significant tissue ischemia — shock; immediate resuscitation
    • > 8.0 mmol/L: Extreme lactic acidosis — very high mortality; ICU mandatory
    • > 12.0 mmol/L: Near-universal mortality without aggressive intervention

SECTION 2: ELECTROLYTE EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

HYPERKALEMIA MANAGEMENT PROTOCOL:
  Step 1 — Stabilise cardiac membrane:
    Calcium gluconate 1g IV over 10 min (if K+ > 6.5 or ECG changes)
    Effect: Immediate (1–3 min); duration 30–60 min

  Step 2 — Shift potassium into cells:
    Insulin (10 units) + Dextrose 50% (50 mL) IV — onset 20 min; duration 4–6h
    Sodium bicarbonate (if metabolic acidosis present)
    Albuterol nebulization (adjunct)

  Step 3 — Eliminate potassium:
    Sodium polystyrene sulfonate or patiromer (bowel resin)
    Furosemide (if adequate renal function)
    Hemodialysis (definitive — for severe/refractory cases or renal failure)

HYPONATREMIA MANAGEMENT:
  Correction rate: No faster than 8–10 mEq/L per 24 hours (osmotic demyelination risk)
  Severe symptomatic (seizures, coma): Hypertonic saline 3% NaCl — 150 mL over 20 min
  Target: Raise Na by 4–6 mEq/L in first hour to control acute symptoms

HYPOCALCEMIA EMERGENT TREATMENT:
  Symptomatic (tetany, seizures): IV calcium gluconate 1–2g over 10–20 min
  Followed by continuous calcium infusion until stable

SECTION 3: COMPLETE BLOOD COUNT — REFERENCE AND CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

HEMOGLOBIN AND HEMATOCRIT:
  Adults (male): Hgb 13.5–17.5 g/dL; Hct 41–53%
  Adults (female): Hgb 12.0–15.5 g/dL; Hct 36–46%
  Anemia classification:
    Mild: Hgb 10.0–12.0 (female) / 10.0–13.5 (male)
    Moderate: Hgb 8.0–9.9 g/dL
    Severe: Hgb 6.5–7.9 g/dL
    Life-threatening: Hgb < 6.5 g/dL

MEAN CORPUSCULAR VOLUME (MCV):
  • Normal: 80–100 fL
  • Microcytic (< 80 fL): Iron deficiency, thalassemia, chronic disease
  • Macrocytic (> 100 fL): B12/folate deficiency, alcohol, hypothyroidism, medications

DIFFERENTIAL BLOOD COUNT:
  Neutrophils (normal 45–70%):
    • Neutrophilia > 75%: Bacterial infection, stress, corticosteroids
    • Neutropenia < 45% with absolute count < 1500/µL: Infection risk
    • Bands > 10%: Left shift — active bacterial infection (sepsis)
  
  Lymphocytes (normal 20–45%):
    • Lymphocytosis > 45%: Viral infection, CLL
    • Lymphopenia < 20%: HIV, immunosuppression, sepsis (poor prognosis marker)
  
  Eosinophils (normal 1–4%):
    • Eosinophilia > 5%: Allergy, parasitic infection, asthma
    • Hypereosinophilia > 20%: End-organ damage risk (Löffler syndrome)

SECTION 4: URINALYSIS CRITICAL FINDINGS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PROTEIN:
  • Trace to 1+: May be benign (orthostatic, fever)
  • 2+ to 3+ (> 100 mg/dL): Significant proteinuria — kidney disease or pre-eclampsia
  • 4+ (> 500 mg/dL): Heavy proteinuria — nephrotic syndrome investigation

GLUCOSE IN URINE:
  • Present when serum glucose > 180 mg/dL (renal threshold)
  • Positive dipstick without hyperglycemia: Renal glycosuria (benign or Fanconi syndrome)

NITRITES AND LEUKOCYTE ESTERASE:
  • Both positive: High probability of UTI (sensitivity ~75%; specificity ~82%)
  • Leukocyte esterase positive alone: Pyuria; culture required

SPECIFIC GRAVITY:
  • < 1.003: Very dilute urine — diabetes insipidus or excess fluid intake
  • > 1.030: Very concentrated — dehydration or SIADH
  
BLOOD IN URINE:
  • Positive dipstick (hemoglobin): Hematuria, hemoglobinuria, or myoglobinuria
  • RBC > 5/HPF on microscopy: Significant hematuria — evaluate for nephrolithiasis, glomerulonephritis, malignancy

SECTION 5: RAPID SEVERITY FRAMEWORK — EMERGENCY TRIAGE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION BY NUMBER OF CRITICAL ABNORMALITIES:
  
  LOW SEVERITY (Score 1–3):
    • 0 critical values; mild abnormalities only
    • All vital signs within acceptable range
    • No organ dysfunction markers
    • Management: Outpatient follow-up; repeat labs in 1–4 weeks

  MODERATE SEVERITY (Score 4–6):
    • 1–2 abnormal values outside reference range (not panic values)
    • Single organ system involvement
    • Stable vital signs
    • Management: Hospital observation; same-day specialist consultation

  HIGH SEVERITY (Score 7–8):
    • 1–2 panic/critical values OR multiple significant abnormalities
    • Evidence of 1–2 organ system dysfunction
    • Abnormal vital signs with hemodynamic response
    • Management: Hospital admission; urgent specialist involvement

  CRITICAL SEVERITY (Score 9–10):
    • ≥ 3 critical/panic values OR life-threatening single value
    • Multi-organ dysfunction (≥ 2 organ systems)
    • Hemodynamic instability
    • Management: ICU admission; immediate intervention; multi-disciplinary team activation
