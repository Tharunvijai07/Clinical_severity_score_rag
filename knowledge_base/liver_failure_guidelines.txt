LIVER FAILURE AND HEPATIC DYSFUNCTION CLINICAL GUIDELINES
AASLD/EASL Guidelines — Acute Liver Failure, Chronic Liver Disease, and Cirrhosis

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 1: LIVER FUNCTION TEST INTERPRETATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

ALANINE AMINOTRANSFERASE (ALT — Liver-specific):
  • Normal: 7–56 U/L (males); 7–45 U/L (females)
  • Mild elevation (< 3× ULN): Non-specific — fatty liver, medication, mild hepatitis
  • Moderate elevation (3–10× ULN): Significant hepatocellular injury — evaluate further
  • Severe elevation (> 10× ULN): Severe hepatocellular damage (hepatitis, ischemia, toxin)
  • Critical elevation (> 1000 U/L): Acute severe hepatitis or ischemic hepatitis (shock liver)
  • Very critical (> 3000 U/L): Massive hepatic necrosis — acute liver failure risk

ASPARTATE AMINOTRANSFERASE (AST):
  • Normal: 10–40 U/L
  • AST/ALT ratio > 2:1: Suggests alcoholic liver disease
  • AST/ALT ratio < 1: Suggests viral hepatitis or NAFLD
  • Very high AST (> 1000 U/L): Acute hepatocellular injury — ischemia, toxin, viral

BILIRUBIN (TOTAL):
  • Normal: 0.3–1.2 mg/dL (5–21 µmol/L)
  • Mild elevation: 1.2–3.0 mg/dL — subclinical jaundice
  • Moderate (3.0–10.0 mg/dL): Clinical jaundice — hepatic or cholestatic cause
  • Severe (> 10.0 mg/dL): Significant hepatic dysfunction or cholestasis
  • Critical (> 20.0 mg/dL): Indicates severe hepatic failure or acute cholangitis
  • Very critical (> 30.0 mg/dL): Fulminant hepatic failure; liver transplant evaluation

ALKALINE PHOSPHATASE (ALP):
  • Normal: 44–147 U/L
  • Elevated (> 3× ULN): Cholestatic pattern — bile duct obstruction, primary biliary cholangitis
  • Isolated elevation: Consider bone disease, pregnancy

GAMMA-GLUTAMYL TRANSFERASE (GGT):
  • Normal: 8–61 U/L (males); 5–36 U/L (females)
  • Elevated with ALP: Confirms hepatic origin of ALP elevation
  • Isolated GGT elevation: Alcohol use, medications, fatty liver

ALBUMIN:
  • Normal: 3.5–5.0 g/dL
  • Mild decrease (3.0–3.5 g/dL): Early hepatic synthetic dysfunction or malnutrition
  • Moderate decrease (2.5–3.0 g/dL): Significant hepatic dysfunction
  • Severe decrease (< 2.5 g/dL): Advanced liver disease; ascites risk; critical synthetic failure
  • Critical (< 2.0 g/dL): Severe malnutrition or end-stage liver disease

PROTHROMBIN TIME (PT) / INR:
  • Normal PT: 11–13.5 seconds; INR: 0.9–1.1
  • INR > 1.5: Coagulopathy — reduced hepatic factor synthesis
  • INR > 2.0: Significant coagulopathy — liver disease or vitamin K deficiency
  • INR > 3.0: Severe coagulopathy — acute liver failure criterion met
  • INR > 6.5: King's College Criteria threshold for poor prognosis in acetaminophen-induced ALF

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 2: ACUTE LIVER FAILURE (ALF)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DEFINITION:
  • Rapid development of hepatocellular dysfunction (coagulopathy INR ≥ 1.5)
  • Encephalopathy in a patient without prior liver disease
  • Duration < 26 weeks

CAUSES (by frequency): Acetaminophen toxicity, viral hepatitis (A, B, E),
drug-induced liver injury (DILI), ischemic hepatitis, Wilson's disease

KING'S COLLEGE CRITERIA (acetaminophen-induced ALF — poor prognosis):
  • Arterial pH < 7.3 after resuscitation, OR
  • ALL THREE of:
    - INR > 6.5 (PT > 100 seconds)
    - Creatinine > 3.4 mg/dL
    - Grade III–IV encephalopathy

KING'S COLLEGE CRITERIA (non-acetaminophen ALF):
  • INR > 6.5 (PT > 100 sec), OR
  • THREE or more of:
    - Age < 10 or > 40 years
    - Duration jaundice to encephalopathy > 7 days
    - Bilirubin > 17.5 mg/dL
    - INR > 3.5
    - Unfavorable etiology (Wilson's disease, DILI, seronegative)

SECTION 3: CHILD-PUGH SCORE (CIRRHOSIS SEVERITY)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Parameters and scoring (1–3 points each):

1. BILIRUBIN (total, mg/dL):
   • < 2: 1 point
   • 2–3: 2 points
   • > 3: 3 points

2. ALBUMIN (g/dL):
   • > 3.5: 1 point
   • 2.8–3.5: 2 points
   • < 2.8: 3 points

3. PROTHROMBIN TIME (INR):
   • < 1.7: 1 point
   • 1.7–2.3: 2 points
   • > 2.3: 3 points

4. ASCITES:
   • None: 1 point
   • Mild: 2 points
   • Moderate–severe: 3 points

5. HEPATIC ENCEPHALOPATHY:
   • None: 1 point
   • Grade I–II: 2 points
   • Grade III–IV: 3 points

CHILD-PUGH CLASS:
  • Class A (5–6 points): Well-compensated disease; 1-year survival ~100%
  • Class B (7–9 points): Significant functional compromise; 1-year survival ~80%
  • Class C (10–15 points): Decompensated cirrhosis; 1-year survival ~45%
  Child-Pugh C: Liver transplant evaluation mandatory

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 4: MELD SCORE (MODEL FOR END-STAGE LIVER DISEASE)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Formula: MELD = 3.78 × ln(bilirubin) + 11.2 × ln(INR) + 9.57 × ln(creatinine) + 6.43

MELD SCORE INTERPRETATION:
  • MELD < 10: Low severity; 90-day mortality ~2%
  • MELD 10–19: Moderate; 90-day mortality ~6%
  • MELD 20–29: Moderate-severe; 90-day mortality ~20%
  • MELD 30–39: Severe; 90-day mortality ~53%
  • MELD ≥ 40: Critical; 90-day mortality ~71%
  MELD ≥ 15: Survival benefit from liver transplantation
  MELD ≥ 25: High priority on transplant waitlist

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 5: COMPLICATIONS OF CIRRHOSIS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

HEPATIC ENCEPHALOPATHY:
  Grade I: Trivial lack of awareness, shortened attention
  Grade II: Lethargy, disorientation, obvious personality change
  Grade III: Somnolent but arousable, gross disorientation
  Grade IV: Coma — cannot be awakened

SPONTANEOUS BACTERIAL PERITONITIS (SBP):
  • PMN count in ascitic fluid ≥ 250 cells/µL: Diagnostic
  • First-line treatment: Third-generation cephalosporins (cefotaxime)
  • Prophylaxis: Norfloxacin or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole in high-risk patients

HEPATORENAL SYNDROME (HRS):
  Type 1 (HRS-AKI): Rapidly progressive; creatinine doubling > 2.5 mg/dL in < 2 weeks
  Type 2 (HRS-CKD): Moderate, steady renal failure with refractory ascites
  Diagnosis requires: Cirrhosis + ascites + creatinine > 1.5 mg/dL + no improvement after 48h albumin + no shock + no nephrotoxins

VARICEAL BLEEDING:
  • Active variceal bleeding: Mortality 10–20% per episode
  • Management: Octreotide + endoscopic band ligation + antibiotic prophylaxis
  • TIPS consideration if refractory bleeding
